# Festival Calendar and Special Days

The temple observes a rotating calendar of festival days determined by the **panchang**. The panchang is issued annually and is the sole authority for dates; local variations are reconciled by the head priest.

## Recurring Days

Three types of days recur every lunar month:

- **Ekadashi** (11th day of each lunar fortnight) — extended morning aarti, fasting encouraged for devotees.
- **Purnima** (full moon) — special abhishekam with milk, honey, ghee, and rose water.
- **Amavasya** (new moon) — ancestral rituals, reduced public darshan hours.

On all three, the annadaan is upgraded to a **mahaprasad** and the volunteer roster expands by 4 to 6 people.

## Major Annual Festivals

The major annual festivals observed by the temple, in typical calendar order:

1. Maha Shivaratri — all-night vigil with hourly aarti.
2. Ram Navami — celebration of Lord Rama's birth, extended kirtan.
3. Krishna Janmashtami — midnight abhishekam of the child form of the deity.
4. Ganesh Chaturthi — 10-day observance with daily processions.
5. Navratri — 9-night observance culminating in Vijaya Dashami.
6. Diwali — extended lamp lighting; the temple remains open until midnight.

## Operational Impact

Festival days affect several operational areas:

- **Kitchen**: mahaprasad menu, additional cooking staff, ingredient orders doubled.
- **Darshan**: crowd management team activated; volunteer marshals wear identifying sashes.
- **Cleaning**: additional cleaning shifts scheduled between crowd waves.
- **Security**: temple trust arranges additional volunteers or paid security depending on expected turnout.

The head priest issues a written festival day plan the previous evening, distributed to all department heads before 8:00 PM.
