# Kitchen Operations Manual

The temple kitchen is a distinct operational unit under the head sevayat. Its primary output is **annadaan** — free cooked meals served to any visitor, regardless of caste, religion, or background. A second output is ritual **prasad** for distribution during darshan hours.

## Staffing

The kitchen operates with a mix of paid staff and volunteer sevayats:

- **Head sevayat**: full-time paid, oversees daily operations and reports to the trust board weekly.
- **Cook staff**: 2 to 4 paid cooks depending on the day of the week.
- **Volunteers**: minimum 6, maximum 15, drawn from a rotating roster. Volunteers performing seva in the kitchen must sign in by 5:00 AM.

## Daily Timeline

| Time     | Activity                                                    |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 5:00 AM  | Kitchen opens; volunteers sign in                           |
| 5:30 AM  | Stock check by head sevayat; prasad preparation begins      |
| 7:00 AM  | Annadaan cooking begins; base menu locked in                |
| 11:30 AM | First annadaan serving begins                               |
| 1:30 PM  | Annadaan closes for the day                                 |
| 4:30 PM  | Evening prasad preparation begins                           |
| 7:30 PM  | Evening prasad distribution                                 |
| 9:30 PM  | Kitchen closes; cleaning and stock reset for next day       |

## Menu

The default annadaan menu is:

- Steamed rice
- Dal (varies by day — yellow dal, black gram, mixed)
- One sabzi (seasonal)
- Roti or puri
- A sweet item on festival days

On days marked in the **panchang** as festival days, the menu is upgraded to a **mahaprasad** with additional sweet items and a second sabzi.

## Hygiene Rules

The following rules are non-negotiable and posted on the kitchen entrance:

1. All cooking is performed barefoot after ritual purification.
2. No leather items (belts, shoes, wallets) are permitted inside the kitchen.
3. Ingredients are inspected before use; anything past its shelf life is discarded.
4. Prasad or annadaan remaining after the final serving is not stored. It is either finished by staff on-site or composted respectfully.

## Reporting

The head sevayat records the daily count of meals served in the kitchen register. This count is reported to the trust board every Monday for the previous week. The board tracks the trend and uses it to project ingredient purchases and volunteer roster sizing.
